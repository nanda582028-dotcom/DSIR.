Each of these teachers is loved for ignoring the institution that employs them. *Sister Act 2* is loosely inspired by Iris Stevenson, a choir director at Crenshaw High School in Los Angeles, and the film keeps almost nothing of what made her work possible.

She was a qualified public school employee who got results inside a public school, on a public school salary, under the same conditions as everybody else in the building. The film replaces her with a nightclub singer in a habit, because a professional doing her job well is apparently not a story.

> He who can, does. He who cannot, teaches.
>
> <footer>George Bernard Shaw, 1903</footer>

What the genre asks us to admire is always the same gesture: a rule broken, a syllabus abandoned, a headmaster overruled. It is never the twelfth year of showing up. The teachers we are taught to love in film succeed by working against their school, which is a convenient argument for anyone who would rather not fund one.

There is a version of this film that would be harder to make and better to watch. It would be about the curriculum meeting, the timetable, the parent who does not answer the phone. Nobody has made it, and the reason is not mysterious.
